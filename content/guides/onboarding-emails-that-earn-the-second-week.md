Most onboarding emails are a product tour delivered in instalments. They describe features in the order the product team thinks about them, and they are written as though the reader has already decided to care.

This is a draft article used to check the blog build.

## Find the one thing first

Before writing anything, find the action that separates people who are still there in week two from people who are not. Not a metric that correlates with everything — a specific, completable thing: the first project created, the first teammate invited, the first report exported.

You are looking for something that is:

- **Completable in one sitting.** If it takes a week, it cannot be the goal of a week-one sequence.
- **Genuinely predictive.** Do the people who do it stay, controlling for how motivated they already were?
- **Ownable by an email.** A message can get someone to click into a flow. It cannot get them to change their team's process.

## Then write the shortest possible path to it

Every email in the sequence should be answerable with one action. Three emails that each ask for one thing beat one email that lists six.

> If an onboarding email needs a paragraph to explain why the action matters, the action is probably the wrong one.

A shape that holds up across most products:

| Day | Message | Asks for |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | Welcome, and the one thing | The activation action |
| 2 | The same thing, different angle | The activation action |
| 5 | Proof it works for someone like them | The activation action |
| 8 | What happens next if they have done it | The second action |

Three of those four emails ask for exactly the same thing. That is not a mistake, and it is the part teams cut first.

## Stop the sequence when it is finished

If someone completes the action on day one, the day-two and day-five emails should not arrive. This sounds obvious and it is broken in about half the sequences I have audited — usually because the sequence was built on a schedule rather than on the state of the account.

## What to measure

Not open rate. The sequence has one job: the share of new accounts that complete the activation action within the first week. Everything else is diagnostics for when that number does not move.
